01 / Who gets what

Where does a $1 Club Entry go?

Players can test locally and play ordinary Play Money games for free. An organizer can also offer paid club admission. In this example, the player spends $1 on access, not a table buy-in. The organizer starts with 80¢ and the Club Entry plugin creator with 20¢. Each pays the platform 20% of their portion.

Organizer

Gets 6,400 chips

80¢ less 16¢ leaves 64¢ in club credit. At 10,000 Cash Chips per dollar, that is 6,400 Cash Chips. The organizer can use them in the club but cannot withdraw them.

Creator

Gets $0.16

The Club Entry creator keeps 16¢: the original 20¢ share less a 4¢ platform royalty. Other plugins have their own offer terms.

Platform

Gets $0.20

The DanGG platform is allocated $0.20 total: $0.16 platform royalty from the organizer and $0.04 from the creator share.

03 / Who operates what

Why a separate reseller is optional

A creator can reach organizers through Marketplace without selling the plugin to a distributor. An organizer runs the club and earns its reputation with members. Players find clubs in Organizers, where subscription status, plugin use, activity and member ratings influence discovery. DanGG hosts the marketplace and charges the stated royalty; NervixPay verifies payment.

Does contributing make me a shareholder?

No. An approved paid plugin can earn its creator a share of sales under its published terms. That gives the creator a financial stake in the success of their work, not shares, dividends or voting rights in DanGG. No sales means no sales revenue. The $1 example shows the configured split; end-to-end settlement has been tested with a simulated payment only.

OpenPoker is the software; DanGG is the operator; NervixPay verifies admissions.
